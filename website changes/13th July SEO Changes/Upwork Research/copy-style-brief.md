# Copy Style Brief (Single Source of Truth)

## 1. Who you're writing for
- Primary: founders/builders on social — actively building, overwhelmed by AI/tech noise, tired of "latest stuff" that doesn't help their current project.
- Secondary: SMB owners — practical, time-poor, care about results not tech.
- Both want **leverage, not loss**. Never imply they're missing out or behind.
- Frame everything as "here's a tool/idea you can use right now in what you're already building/running" — never "here's what you don't have yet."
- Tone: a smart friend sharing something useful, not a marketer creating urgency.
- Do: "If you're building X, here's how this helps." "If you're running a shop, here's what this saves you."
- Never: "You're missing out," "you're behind," "don't get left behind," FOMO framing of any kind.

## 2. Structure: hook-first, always start there
- Every piece is Hook → Meat → CTA (modern AIDA). Hook = attention, Meat = mechanism + belief + proof, CTA = one action.
- Hook rule (First-Line-Second-Line): line 1 creates an incomplete thought/tension; line 2 pays it off. Never open with feature or product name.
- Best hooks name a specific, felt moment ("You're selling 'strategy,' but clients are buying chaos") — not a vague question ("Struggling to scale?").
- Anchor check: title + first sentence set the frame for everything after. Lead with a human moment or concrete scenario, never an abstract concept.
- **When to deviate from hook-first:** long-form narrative/brand pieces (founder stories, manifestos) can open on the Wall or Epiphany beat instead and flash back — but still open on tension, never on a feature list or company description.
- Every piece is secretly a story: Character → Desire → Wall → Epiphany → Plan → Conflict → Achievement → Transformation. Compress to Character → Wall → Epiphany → Result for short-form. No Wall (real friction) = no reader investment.

## 3. Scannability (non-negotiable)
- One idea per line. Generous whitespace.
- No paragraph over two lines — if longer, split it.
- Burrito test: pull one sentence out of a paragraph; if the paragraph still works without it, cut that sentence.
- Kaplan's Law: any word not working for you is working against you. Cut until you can't cut more without losing meaning.
- Sentences ~18 words average, active voice ("We ship in 48 hours," not "It is shipped in 48 hours").
- Write at a 4th–6th grade reading level. If you need a thesaurus, don't use the word.
- Read it aloud — if it sounds like a lecture, not a conversation, rewrite it.
- One claim per line/section — cut "and," "also," "plus." Narrow scope = stronger claim ("We make jeans. That's it.").

## 4. Core frameworks to apply (pick what fits, don't stack all at once)

**A. Three Rules Filter (every headline/CTA/subject line)**
Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say it? All three "yes" or rewrite.
Zoom-in: replace abstractions (seamless, optimize, leverage, transform) with a concrete, visualizable action.

**B. Don't sell the drill, sell the hole (features → benefit → emotion)**
Feature → "So what?" → benefit → "How does that feel?" → emotion. Never list specs; state what the reader gets to stop doing or start feeling.

**C. Proof stacking (every promise needs proof)**
Bigger the claim, bigger the proof burden. Use: hard numbers, named examples, before/after, customer quotes with context, mechanism (why it's true). Drop "proof breadcrumbs" (a number/name/constraint) every 5–10 lines in longer copy. Weak proof → tighten the claim instead of inflating it.

**D. Frame Shift / Dissonance (for counterintuitive or belief-changing copy)**
Never attack a worldview head-on. Validate what they already believe → establish shared pain inside that worldview → introduce your idea as a small modification → root it with examples → let them arrive at your conclusion feeling like it was their own idea. Optionally use dissonance: present two things they already believe that conflict, then offer your point as the resolution.

## 5. Awareness-level matching (diagnose before writing a word)
| Reader state | What they need | Hook style |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware (doesn't know the problem) | Agitate a symptom they feel but can't name | "Why do most [role] feel [pain] even when they try hard?" |
| Problem aware (knows problem, not solutions) | Validate the pain, tease a solution exists | "If you're struggling with X, here's why it's not your fault" |
| Solution aware (knows solutions, not yours) | Position your mechanism as the superior path | "Most tools fail because they ignore [insight]. Here's what works." |
| Product aware (knows you, hasn't bought) | Handle objections, strengthen desire | "Still on the fence? Here's what happened when [name] said yes." |
| Most aware (past customers/warm audience) | New offer, upgrade, referral | "Since you've already done X, this is the next logical step." |

Biggest failure mode: leading with product features for an Unaware reader — they don't know they need it yet, so educate the problem first. Always ask: who exactly is reading this, and what do they already believe?

## 6. Avoid list (instant rewrite triggers)
- Fear/FOMO words: "don't miss out," "you're behind," "before it's too late," "act now," fake countdown urgency.
- Hype/abstract filler: "seamless," "unlock," "supercharge," "revolutionize," "game-changing," "next-level," "cutting-edge," "empower," "transform," "leverage" (as a verb), "utilize," "synergy," "robust," "innovative" (used as a claim, not a fact).
- Adverb padding: "really," "very," "highly," "extremely" — delete or replace with proof.
- Hedging: "perhaps," "maybe," "seems," "might" — be definitive or don't say it.
- Generic-AI tells: on-the-nose symmetry ("It's not just X, it's Y"), listy triads with no proof, superlatives with no defensible axis ("best," "most powerful"), claims a competitor could paste their logo onto.
- Passive voice and corporate/boardroom phrasing — if you wouldn't say it to a friend over coffee, rewrite it.
- Balanced, hedge-everything takes ("on one hand... on the other") — pick a side; the market remembers sharp edges, not balance.

## 7. Final check before publishing
- Can a stranger get the point in 2 seconds?
- Does the hook match the reader's actual awareness level?
- Is there a real Wall (friction) in the story, not just a happy result?
- Every adjective — could you point at proof instead of asserting it?
- Would a competitor's logo also fit under this headline? If yes, rewrite.
